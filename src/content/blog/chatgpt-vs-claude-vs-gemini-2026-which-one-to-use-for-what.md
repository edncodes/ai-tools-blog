## TL;DR

All three are close enough on raw ability in 2026 that "which one is smartest" is the wrong question. The useful question is "which one fits the work in front of me." The short answers:

- **ChatGPT** is the widest toolbox. It has the most features, apps and integrations under one subscription, and it's the safest single pick if you want one tool that covers a bit of everything. ([ChatGPT pricing](https://openai.com/chatgpt/pricing/))
- **Claude** is the one for long, careful work. It gives you a 1M-token context window, the best-liked writing, and the coding agent people rate highest in Claude Code. ([Claude models overview](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/about-claude/models/overview))
- **Gemini** is the one that lives inside Google. It also runs a 1M-token window, and it's the strongest of the three at video and at working across Gmail, Docs and Drive. ([Google AI plans](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/))
- You don't have to pick one. Each has a genuinely useful free tier, and the three are good at different things, so the cheapest real upgrade is usually to add a second free tier rather than pay for a bigger plan on the one you already have.
- The detail that bites isn't the model, it's the plan. Every one of these has a free tier, a roughly $20 tier, and a $100-plus tier, and the things that actually limit you (message caps, context size, agent access) change between them. Read the plan ladder before you read the model names.

## The 30-second answer

If you only want a starting point, here's where most people should begin:

| You want to... | Start with | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Write and edit long documents | Claude | Users consistently rate its writing and tone highest |

## Why "which model is best" is the wrong question

For most of the last two years, "which AI is best" had a moving answer based on whichever model topped the latest benchmark. In 2026 that question has mostly stopped being useful, for three reasons.

![Diagram showing ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini converging into one capability band](/images/blog/model-capability-convergence.png)

First, the three have converged. OpenAI's current line is the [GPT-6 family](https://openai.com/chatgpt/overview/), Anthropic's is [Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 4.5](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/about-claude/models/overview), and Google's is [Gemini 4 Argon](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/). On the tasks most people actually do, all three land in the same band, and the leader flips with each release. If you choose on benchmark order, you'll re-choose every month.

Second, the names tell you almost nothing. A model called "GPT-6 Astra" or "Fable 5.1" or "Gemini 4 Argon" is a marketing label for a build. What matters to you is what the tool can reach (your files, your email, your code), how much it can hold in view at once, and whether it does the thing without you babysitting it.

Third, the differences that actually change your day are small and specific: where the tool lives, how big a context window you get on the plan you'd pay for, how it talks to you, and what the next tier up costs. So the rest of this post compares them on those axes, not on a scoreboard. If you want the deeper version of "use two together to cover each other's blind spots," that's [exactly what this post on combining ChatGPT and Claude covers](/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-and-claude-together-to-get-better-results).

## ChatGPT: the widest toolbox

ChatGPT is no longer just a chat box. OpenAI now splits it into three surfaces that share one account and one model picker: **Chat** for questions and everyday help, **Work** for completing documents, slides, spreadsheets and reports, and **Codex** for writing, testing and shipping code. ([ChatGPT overview](https://openai.com/chatgpt/overview/))

On capability, the current flagship is **GPT-6 Astra**, which OpenAI says improved in coding, research, computer use and multi-step work, and can build documents that follow your own templates. It sits alongside GPT-6 Sol and Luna and the older GPT-5.6 line. ChatGPT's standout features are the ones you can't get in the same place anywhere else:

- **Deep Research**, which hunts through hundreds of sources over five to thirty minutes and returns a cited report. ([OpenAI deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/))
- **Agent mode and Dots**, always-on agents that keep working between conversations. Dots are a Pro feature.
- **Projects**, Spaces and **Custom GPTs** (which OpenAI is migrating into plugins). ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes))
- **Image and video**, via ChatGPT Images 2.5 and Sora, plus Voice with video.
- **Connectors** to Slack, Google and Microsoft apps, and Microsoft 365 add-ins for Excel, Word and PowerPoint.

Where it's weakest, per its own users, is tone and restraint. More on that below.

## Claude: the long-work and writing specialist

Claude is Anthropic's assistant, and its pitch is deliberately different from ChatGPT's: point it at files and tools and hand it a task, rather than only asking questions. It runs on web, iOS, Android and desktop. ([Claude product overview](https://claude.com/product/overview))

Its 2026 line-up is four models that trade speed against power: **Fable 5.1** for the hardest long-horizon reasoning, **Opus 5.5** as the recommended default for agentic coding and knowledge work, **Sonnet 5.5** for the best balance of speed and intelligence, and **Haiku 4.5** as the fastest. Three of them carry a **1M-token context window**, which is the headline number here. ([Claude models overview](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/about-claude/models/overview))

What Claude is known for:

- **Long, messy documents.** Users point to it turning sprawling files and email threads into clean output without losing the top of the document by the bottom.
- **Writing quality.** Across review sites, its tone and lack of sycophancy are the most-cited strengths.
- **Claude Code**, its terminal, IDE, web and mobile coding agent, which reads issues, writes code, runs tests and opens pull requests. It's included on every paid plan. ([Claude Code](https://claude.com/product/claude-code))
- **Artifacts, Projects and MCP connectors**, plus file and data analysis across most common formats.

**Claude pricing** ([Claude plans](https://claude.com/pricing)):

| Plan | Price | Notable limits and access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Chat, web search, Artifacts, memory, connectors; Projects up to 5; Sonnet and Haiku models |
| Pro | $17/mo annual ($200 up front) or $20/mo | Claude Code, Projects, Research, more models, Claude in Chrome and Microsoft 365 |
| Max 5x | $100/mo | 5x Pro usage; higher output limits; priority access |
| Max 20x | $200/mo | 20x Pro usage |

| Enterprise | $20/seat/mo + usage at API rates | SCIM, audit logs, role-based access, HIPAA-ready option |

One caution the pricing page makes explicit: usage limits reset on a rolling five-hour window, and your work across chat and Claude Code draws from the same pool. That's the most common complaint about Claude, and it's worth reading before you pick a tier.

## Gemini: the one inside Google

Gemini is Google's assistant and model family, built on Google DeepMind's Gemini line. Its current frontier model, **Gemini 4 Argon**, launched on 30 September 2026 and Google calls it an industry leader in reasoning, agentic coding and long-context work, with a **1M-token input limit and a 1M-token output limit**. ([Google DeepMind, Gemini](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/))

Where Gemini stands out:

- **Native multimodality and video.** It understands and generates across text, image, audio and video, and its **Omni** video model does ten-second clips with native audio, photo-to-video and video-to-video editing. ([Gemini video](https://gemini.google/overview/veo/))
- **Google Workspace integration.** It reaches Gmail, Docs and Drive, plus Calendar, Tasks and Keep, and appears inside the Workspace apps themselves. ([Google Workspace connector help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15229592))
- **Deep Research and Canvas**, where reports become apps, quizzes, infographics or audio overviews. ([Gemini Deep Research](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/))
- **Gems, now becoming Skills**, reusable assistants you can stack in one prompt.

**Gemini pricing** ([Google AI plans](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/)):

A note that matters if you're comparing on price: the Google plans bundle storage, YouTube and other perks, so the $19.99 tier isn't only an AI subscription. That makes it hard to compare to ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro dollar for dollar.

## Context windows: the number that quietly limits you

Context window is how much text a model can hold in view at once, and it's the most under-discussed spec in consumer AI because it changes what you can even attempt.

![Bar chart comparing 2026 context windows across ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini plans](/images/blog/context-window-comparison.png)

On paper, Claude and Gemini both offer up to 1M tokens on paid plans, while ChatGPT's paid context ranges from 128K on its instant model to 400K on its reasoning model. In practice, that means dumping a 300-page document into Claude or Gemini is routine, while ChatGPT's limit depends on which model you pick and which plan you're on.

The trap is that the free and cheap tiers are much smaller. On ChatGPT's Free plan the instant model holds 27K tokens, roughly twelve pages. If large files are your use case, the plan you choose matters more than the brand.

## What real users say

The picture from actual users is more useful than any spec sheet, and it's consistent: each tool has a personality, and the same tool that delights one person annoys another.

**Gemini's fans are loudest about multimodality and documents.** In a long Reddit thread asking why people prefer Gemini, the top reply makes the case through a specific example: for programming videos on YouTube, ChatGPT can't analyze video at all, while Gemini can generate a timestamped index of a finished video automatically — a gap the commenter called an objective fact, not a matter of taste.

A reply further down the same thread complicates that story: it argues that the loudest Gemini praise online mostly comes from people already frustrated with ChatGPT, and that many of them haven't actually paid for Gemini themselves.

**ChatGPT's complaints are about guardrails and tone.** The sharpest version comes from OpenAI's own community forum, where a long thread about GPT-5's behaviour collects frustration with the safety layer, summed up in one line: "too many guardrails, not being treated like adults, too much censorship."

On G2, one student reviewer described a softer, more common failure: ChatGPT will sometimes misread the actual problem and answer with full confidence anyway, then simply apologize and offer another guess when corrected, without really diagnosing where it went wrong.

**Claude's praise is about writing and context, its criticism about limits and price.** A G2 reviewer summed up the writing case by saying Claude is particularly good at turning "long and messy documents" into clean, well-structured output that needs less editing afterward.

A paying user's complaint tells the other side: even on the Pro plan, heavy use — especially through Claude Code — burns through usage limits quickly, which makes the overall cost feel higher than its competitors once you're actually relying on it daily.

One honest caveat about review sites: Claude's G2 score (4.6) and its Trustpilot score (1.5/5 from over 2,000 reviews) sit at opposite ends of the scale. G2 skews toward professional software buyers; Trustpilot skews toward consumers complaining about billing and limits. Read both, and read the plan you're actually considering.

## The hidden cost: it's the plan ladder, not the logo

Here's the part the model names hide. All three use the same three-step ladder, and the step you're on decides what you can do:

- **Free** is real, and enough to test each one properly. It's how you should decide between them before paying anything.
- **Around $20** is where the useful stuff unlocks: bigger context, more messages, and access to the reasoning models. This is the tier most individuals should buy, and it's the tier where all three are closest in price.
- **$100 and up** is for people who work in the tool all day. ChatGPT Pro starts at $100, Claude Max at $100, Google AI Ultra at $99.99, and the top tiers run to $200 or $500 a month.

![Three cards comparing what $20 a month buys on ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro and Gemini AI Pro](/images/blog/plan-ladder-comparison.png)

Two traps sit in that ladder. First, the free tiers shrink over time as companies push users upward, so a free tier you relied on last year may not hold. Second, the $100 tiers are priced for heavy professional use, not curiosity. If you're an individual, the honest advice is to stay on a $20 tier and add a free tier of a second tool rather than paying for a bigger plan on one.

## How to use all three together

You don't have to choose. The reason to use more than one is that they fail differently: a model that's confidently wrong on one task is often right when a second model checks it, and the free tiers make this almost free to try.

![Diagram routing three kinds of task to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, then combining all three into one workflow](/images/blog/multi-model-workflow-routing.png)

A simple way to start: use one tool as your main workspace (the one whose ecosystem you already live in) and bring in a second only for its strength. Draft in Claude if writing is the task, research in Gemini if it needs your Gmail and Drive, and keep ChatGPT as the general-purpose hub. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is better in 2026, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

None of them wins across the board. ChatGPT has the widest set of features and apps, Claude is the strongest for long documents and careful writing, and Gemini is the best value if you already live in Google apps. The useful question is which one fits the task in front of you, not which tops a ranking.

### Can I use ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini for free?

Yes. All three have free tiers that are genuinely usable, and testing them for free is the cheapest way to decide before you pay anything. The limits show up when you want a bigger context window, more messages or the reasoning models, which sit on the paid tiers. Compare them on the [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/chatgpt/pricing/), [Claude](https://claude.com/pricing) and [Google AI](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/) pricing pages.

### Should I use more than one AI assistant at the same time?

Often, yes. The three fail in different ways, so a second model checking important work catches more mistakes than either alone, and each free tier makes it almost free to try. For the step-by-step version, read [How to use ChatGPT and Claude together to get better results](/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-and-claude-together-to-get-better-results).

### How much should I pay for an AI assistant?

For most individuals, around $20 a month is the sweet spot: it unlocks the bigger context window, more messages and the reasoning models. The $100-plus tiers are priced for people who work in the tool all day, not for curiosity. If you are unsure, stay on a $20 tier and add a free tier of a second tool instead of paying for a bigger plan on one.

### What is the biggest hidden limit of these plans?

Usage limits and context windows, not the model names. Claude's limits reset on a rolling five-hour window and are shared between chat and Claude Code, ChatGPT's context drops to around 27K tokens on the free plan, and the free tiers tend to shrink over time. Read the plan ladder before you read the model names.
